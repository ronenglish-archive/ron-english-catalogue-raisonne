Biography

Ron English is an American artist born on June 3, 1959, in Decatur, Illinois. Over a career spanning more than four decades, he has worked across painting, drawing, sculpture, printmaking, photography, design, street art, public art, toys, publishing, music, and digital media, building a practice that is difficult to confine to a single category.

Often described as the “Godfather of Street Art,” English emerged as an early and influential figure in culture jamming and billboard intervention, beginning unauthorized billboard projects in the early 1980s. He later coined the term “Popaganda” for a body of work that draws together advertising, politics, art history, consumer culture, humor, and popular imagery. His public works and interventions have often used the visual authority of advertising itself as a way to question the messages embedded in mass culture.

Artist Overview

English’s practice has never been confined to a single medium, setting, or mode of expression. His paintings are known for their highly finished technique, vivid color, and elaborate compositions, while his street work introduced many of the same ideas into public space on billboards and murals. Rather than separating these activities, English has described painting and street art as complementary parts of his life as an artist.

A central part of his work is the transformation of familiar imagery. Advertising, corporate mascots, political figures, art history, cartoons, and other elements of shared visual culture are reworked into images that can be comic, unsettling, satirical, or surreal. But English’s work is not limited to appropriation. Over decades he has also built an extensive visual mythology of his own, with recurring characters, archetypes, invented environments, and narratives that continue to evolve across different bodies of work and media. Delusionville is one of the fullest expressions of that world-building, expanding those characters into a fictional society with its own mythology, politics, and social order.

What connects the different parts of English’s career is less a single style or medium than a consistent way of looking at the world: questioning familiar images, testing the authority of cultural symbols, using humor to approach serious subjects, and continually inventing new forms through which those ideas can take shape. His work has moved from illegal billboards to museum and gallery painting, from sculpture and collectible figures to books, music, film, and digital projects, without abandoning the experimental range that has characterized his practice from the beginning.
